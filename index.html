The reason shops don't go online

It isn't the website. It's what the website does to the accounts.

The usual outcome

Two inventories that drift

The web shop keeps its own stock numbers. Tally keeps the real ones. Within a week they disagree, and nobody can say which is right. Staff end up re-typing every online order into Tally by hand.

And then

The same saree, sold twice

A walk-in customer buys the piece on the rack. Thirty seconds later the website sells it too. One of those two people is about to be disappointed, and it's usually the one who paid online.

How a sale happens

From a photograph on WhatsApp to a voucher in Tally, with no human in the middle.

  1. She sends a photoFrom a wedding, a shop window, Instagram. No app to install, no search terms to guess.
  2. The AI reads the sareeFabric, weave, colour palette, motifs, border and pallu work, occasion — extracted by Gemini vision.
  3. Your stock is searchedThose attributes become a 768-dimension vector, matched against your catalogue. Only items actually in stock come back.
  4. She picks and checks outProduct cards with price and MRP, then name and address, all inside the chat. Order number issued.
  5. Tally receives the saleA GST-compliant sales voucher, stock decremented, customer ledger mapped. Automatically.

The Tally bridge

Tally runs on a desktop inside the shop, on localhost:9000, behind a consumer router. No cloud service can reach it. So the shop PC reaches out instead, and never accepts a connection from outside.

Most Tally integrations fail in the same four ways. Each one is handled deliberately:

What goes wrong
What this does
Tally's XML engine is single-threaded. Two requests at once and it freezes or returns Server Busy.
Serialised queue — concurrency of one, 250 ms spacing, exponential-backoff retry. It cannot be flooded.
A network retry posts the invoice twice. The books now show a sale that never happened.
Idempotent vouchers — every voucher is keyed to its order number, so a replay is a no-op.
The wrong company file is open and the integration writes into someone else's accounts.
GUID binding — the company is cryptographically bound to the shop. A mismatch refuses to sync rather than guessing.
An unpaid or abandoned order still posts, inflating sales and GST liability.
Prepaid only — a voucher is raised exclusively for orders marked paid.

What the shop gets

One dashboard, and a stock rack that can't be sold twice.

Godown isolation

Online stock is quarantined

Web sales draw from a dedicated Tally godown. The counter and the website physically cannot sell the same piece. Each billing terminal is locked to one godown and refused if it asks for another.

Terminal pairing

A six-character code

A new billing PC displays an activation code. The owner enters it once from their phone, names the terminal and picks its godown. A terminal can bill — it cannot delete products or adjust stock.

Dashboard

Catalogue, orders, discrepancies

Stock per godown, variant grouping, order pipeline, customer directory, sales analytics, and a review queue for any mismatch between Tally and the cloud.

See what the AI saw

Every customer photo, the attributes extracted from it, and the items it matched — so a bad recommendation can be explained rather than guessed at.
